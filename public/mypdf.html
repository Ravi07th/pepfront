1

Tell me about yourself.

Short Answer

Give a 60–90 second summary focusing on your education, key IT projects, internships, and what you want to do next.

Detailed Answer

Start with your current academic status (degree, college), mention 1–2 technical projects (what you built, tech stack, outcome), any internship or part-time role (what you learned), and end with what you want to contribute to the role (example: build scalable backend services or improve frontend UX).

Example
“I’m a final-year B.Tech CSE student from XYZ University. My final-year project was a realtime chat app using React, Node.js, and WebSocket which supported 200+ concurrent users. I interned at ABC where I wrote automation scripts in Python to reduce manual testing effort by 40%. I’m excited to apply my full-stack and automation skills to entry-level engineering challenges.”
Tips (IT fresher)
• Mention measurable project outcomes (users, speedups, reduced bugs).
• Name exact technologies (React/Node/Mongo/Python).
• Keep it under 90 seconds — practice and record yourself.
2

Why do you want to work for our company?

Short Answer

Show company-specific research + a match between your skills/values and the role.

Detailed Answer

Mention one or two things about the company — product, culture, recent initiative — and connect them to your background. Example: interest in their cloud offerings because you built cloud-hosted projects or took related courses.

Example
“I admire [Company]'s focus on developer tools. During my internship I worked on API design and I’m excited to work at a company building scalable APIs where I can apply and learn modern cloud practices.”
Tips (IT fresher)
• Reference a specific product, blog post, or tech stack used by the company.
• Explain how your recent project/internship prepared you to contribute.
• Avoid generic praise — be specific and factual.
3

What are your strengths?

Short Answer

Pick 2–3 strengths relevant to IT (problem-solving, debugging, quick learner) and back them with short examples.

Detailed Answer

Explain each strength briefly and attach an example (project/internship/hackathon). For example, “problem-solving — used algorithms to optimize search time by X%” or “teamwork — led 3-person project using agile sprints.”

Example
“I’m strong at debugging and optimization — I reduced query time in my project by indexing DB columns and caching responses, improving latency by 2×.”
Tips (IT fresher)
• Use tech-specific evidence: frameworks, performance gains, PRs merged.
• Avoid one-word answers — show the context and impact.
4

What are your weaknesses?

Short Answer

Pick a real (non-critical) weakness and show steps you took to improve it.

Detailed Answer

Example: “I used to avoid detailed documentation. I now allocate time each sprint to write clear README and API docs and use inline comments to help teammates.” This shows growth and responsibility.

Example
“Weakness: I was uncomfortable with public speaking. Action: I joined college tech clubs and presented twice; now I can demo projects confidently.”
Tips (IT fresher)
• Don’t say something essential to the job (e.g., “I’m bad at coding”).
• Show measurable improvement or a concrete plan to improve.
5

Where do you see yourself in 5 years?

Short Answer

Show realistic growth: technical contributor → senior / specialist → mentor; align with company career paths.

Detailed Answer

Mention learning goals (cloud certifications, full-stack mastery, domain expertise). Express willingness to grow into roles that add more ownership (lead developer, tech lead).

Tips (IT fresher)
• Be specific but flexible: mention technologies and roles you want to master.
• Avoid overly-ambitious titles (CEO) — show a growth mindset instead.
6

Why should we hire you?

Short Answer

Summarize top skills, projects, and eagerness to learn — tailored to the job description.

Detailed Answer

Mention 2–3 strengths that match the role; give a quick project that shows you delivered results. Emphasize culture fit and coachability.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Mirror keywords from the job description (e.g., REST APIs, React, unit tests).
• Use a recent project/internship as proof of capability.
7

Tell me about a challenge you faced and how you overcame it.

Short Answer

Describe the technical challenge, your specific actions, and measurable outcome.

Detailed Answer

E.g., debugging a memory leak in a Java app: how you profiled, identified the leak, fixed resource handling, and validated the fix with load tests.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Focus on steps you personally owned and the technical tools used (profilers, logs, tests).
• Quantify impact (reduced crashes, improved CPU/memory usage).
8

Describe a time you worked in a team.

Short Answer

Explain your role, how you collaborated, and the result.

Detailed Answer

Mention communication tools (Git, Jira, Slack), how you split tasks, resolved conflicts, and integrated code (PRs, code reviews) to deliver the project.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Focus on concrete collaboration actions: code reviews, CI pipeline set-up, testing responsibilities.
• Explain any mentorship or leadership you provided, even informally.
9

How do you handle pressure?

Short Answer

Describe organization and prioritization techniques and give an example of delivering under a deadline.

Detailed Answer

Use examples such as triaging bugs, creating small deliverables, using version control to keep work safe, and communicating timelines to stakeholders.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Mention task breakdown, timeboxing, and automated tests to reduce risk.
• Demonstrate calm, methodical problem solving in the example.
10

What do you know about our products/services?

Short Answer

Mention core product lines, target customers, and a technical detail if possible (APIs, platform, stack).

Detailed Answer

Show you researched: cite a product/feature, public blog, or GitHub repo. Relate it to your skills (e.g., “You use Kubernetes; I completed a mini-project deploying services to k8s”).

Tips (IT fresher)
• Check company blog, careers page, and product docs.
• If possible, try a demo or signup to experience the product firsthand.
11

Are you willing to relocate?

Short Answer

Be honest; if yes, show flexibility; if not, explain constraints.

Detailed Answer

If willing, mention timeline and willingness to manage logistics. If remote-first, ask about hybrid opportunities and relocation support.

Tips (IT fresher)
• If unsure, ask about remote/hybrid roles and relocation assistance.
• Keep the answer crisp — logistical details can be discussed later.
12

What motivates you?

Short Answer

Frame motivation around learning, solving technical problems, and building impact for users.

Detailed Answer

Give examples: debugging tricky issues, improving performance, shipping a feature that users love, or learning new frameworks/certifications.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Tie motivation to day-to-day tasks relevant to the role (coding, design, testing).
• Avoid cliched answers — personalize with a recent example of what drove you.
13

Describe your ideal work environment.

Short Answer

Mention collaboration, mentorship, clear processes, and focus on learning.

Detailed Answer

Example: small cross-functional teams, code reviews, CI/CD pipelines, and frequent feedback cycles that help you learn and ship code safely.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Align your answer with signals in the company's culture (startup vs enterprise).
• Show eagerness for mentorship and growth opportunities.
14

How do you prioritize tasks?

Short Answer

Use impact vs effort, deadlines, and stakeholder needs to prioritize tasks.

Detailed Answer

Explain simple techniques: create a task list, estimate effort, pick high-impact tasks first, and keep teammates updated. Use tools like Trello/Jira and small iterations.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Mention any agile practice you used (sprints, standups).
• Emphasize communication when priorities change.
15

Give an example of a time you showed leadership.

Short Answer

Leadership can be informal: leading a mini-team, organizing sprints, or mentoring juniors.

Detailed Answer

Describe the situation, your actions (delegation, planning, review), and positive outcomes (on-time delivery, improved code quality).

Tips (IT fresher)
• Use project examples where you coordinated work, set deadlines, or led demos.
• Highlight soft skills alongside technical contributions.
16

What is your greatest achievement?

Short Answer

Pick an achievement that demonstrates technical skill and impact.

Detailed Answer

Describe the problem, your role, technologies used, and measurable result (performance gains, user adoption, awards, hackathon wins).

Tips (IT fresher)
• Prefer outcomes with numbers (users, speed improvements, etc.).
• Link how this achievement prepares you for the role.
17

How do you handle criticism?

Short Answer

Accept feedback, analyze it, and apply improvements; show example of change after feedback.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Give an example where code reviews improved your code.
• Demonstrate openness to learning and iteration.
18

Tell me about a time you failed.

Short Answer

Be honest, explain learnings and how you avoided repeat mistakes.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Choose a failure in process or scope, not integrity. Show concrete steps you took to improve.
19

What do you know about our competitors?

Short Answer

Show awareness of the landscape and where the company stands technically or in product features.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Focus on product/technical differentiators rather than opinions.
20

How do you stay updated in your field?

Short Answer

Mention blogs, newsletters, GitHub, courses, coding practice, and meetups.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Mention specifics: Hacker News, freeCodeCamp, MDN, Coursera, GitHub stars, weekly coding on LeetCode.
21

Why did you choose your field of study?

Short Answer

Share curiosity or specific experiences that motivated you toward IT (projects, mentors, competitions).

Tips (IT fresher)
• Add a short anecdote: first project, first code that excited you, or a mentor influence.
22

How do you manage deadlines?

Short Answer

Plan backwards from the deadline, prioritize, and communicate blockers early.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Mention version control best practices (feature branches, incremental commits) and CI tests to avoid last-minute regressions.
23

What are your salary expectations?

Short Answer

Give a range based on research and show flexibility for a good role/learning opportunities.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Research market rates for freshers in your city; mention willingness to discuss after understanding role and growth.
24

What is your preferred working style?

Short Answer

Be flexible: comfortable with both independent deep work and collaborative environments.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Give examples: individual tasks (feature coding) vs collaborative tasks (pair programming, design reviews).
25

Describe a time you went above and beyond.

Short Answer

Show initiative: added tests, improved docs, automated deployments, or helped teammates after hours.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Concrete technical examples are best: quick prototype, automated CI, or debugging a production issue during crunch time.
26

What are your hobbies?

Short Answer

Prefer hobbies that show helpful soft skills (problem solving, creativity) — but any genuine hobby is fine.

Tips (IT fresher)
• If you have tech-related hobbies (open-source contributions, side projects), mention them and link GitHub if asked.
27

How do you adapt to new technologies?

Short Answer

Talk about structured learning: documentation → tutorials → small projects → apply in a real project.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Mention a quick learning example: learned Docker/Kubernetes and deployed a sample service within a week.
28

Do you prefer working alone or in a team?

Short Answer

Say you can do both and prefer the one that best suits the task; give examples for each.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Give examples: individual tasks (feature dev) and team tasks (release, architecture design).
29

How do you deal with conflicts at work?

Short Answer

Listen, clarify facts, propose solutions, and escalate when needed.

Tips (IT fresher)
• Show an example where you mediated a technical disagreement by proposing an experiment or a small POC.
30

Any questions for us?

Short Answer

Always ask — it shows interest. Ask about onboarding, tech stack, mentorship, or a recent product decision.

Suggested Questions
• What would a typical first 3 months look like for this role?
• What are your expectations for the first project I’d work on?
• How does the team handle code reviews and testing?
